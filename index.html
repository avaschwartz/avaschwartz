Unit 1 summative

From a blinking LED to playing notes

I took digital output one step further: a piezo buzzer and a keypad on an Arduino Uno. Press a key on the keypad to hear the note it sends to the buzzer.

The demo here is in your browser, so you can try it with a mouse, a touch screen, or your keyboard.

tone(10, ---);
Press a key to play a note

Keyboard works too: 0-9, A-D, * and #.

The skill I built on, and why

The skill I am building on is digital output. In class, I learned how to control an Arduino by making an LED flash off and on. I wanted to take this a step further by making the Arduino control something more interesting than just an LED. So, I decided to learn how to use a piezo buzzer.

I chose this because I was interested in how Arduino can combine electronics and programming to create something interactive. I also wanted to learn how different notes can be created using code.

The new components and where I learned them

The new components I used were a piezo buzzer and a keypad. I researched how to connect the piezo buzzer to an Arduino and learned that the tone() function can make the buzzer play different frequencies. Higher or lower frequencies create different musical notes.

Sources: ChatGPT and Arduino

What I tried, and what didn't work

At first, I did not understand how to make two lights work at different times with the wiring on the Arduino Breadboard, so I played around with it until I found that the wires have to go into different sides of the breadboard. Another thing that did not work at first was the keypad would not sync up to the Arduino Software, so it took a lot of failures to connect the keypad.
